# Product notes

## Principle

The route is the product. London Drift should produce a coherent wander that a person would not naturally make themselves. POIs are seasoning, not a checklist.

## V1 user

A person already in London with 30–120 minutes, a starting point and a desired mood. They should get a route in seconds, without creating an account or understanding the algorithm.

## Decisions

- Point-to-point is the default because it creates stronger neighbourhood transitions.
- Map and route controls share the first viewport; there is no marketing hero.
- Modes are weight configurations over one algorithm.
- The first map treatment borrows from printed A–Z and field-guide graphics: paper, ink, restrained red, editorial serif and compact labels.
- Route explanations are short and specific. Stops remain sparse.
- LLMs are excluded from core routing.

## Deliberately postponed

Authentication, full navigation, social feeds, challenges, badges, weather, health integrations, public route creation and city expansion. These are secondary to route quality.

## V2 priorities

1. Personal novelty based on canonical street segments.
2. A defensible London exploration map and percentage.
3. Sunset, daylight and weather signals for photography and night modes.
4. Continue-from-endpoint and end-near-destination constraints.
5. Feedback-driven personal weight adjustments.
